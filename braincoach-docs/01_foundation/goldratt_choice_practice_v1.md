## Purpose

BrainCoach frequently needs to make choices:

* strategic choices;
* offer choices;
* GPS positioning choices;
* content choices;
* interview-question choices;
* parent-facing language choices;
* product-design choices;
* agent-reasoning choices.

Choice must become a trained practice for humans and agents.

The purpose is not to quote Goldratt.

The purpose is to convert choice into a repeatable thinking discipline.

## Core Principle

Choice is not preference selection.

Choice is disciplined reasoning under constraint.

BrainCoach should choose by clarifying:

```text
Goal
↓
Current reality
↓
Constraint
↓
Conflict / contradiction
↓
Assumptions
↓
Invalid assumption
↓
Better option
↓
Reality feedback
```

## Choice Is Trainable

Clear thinking is a skill.

Agents and humans must train choice by practicing:

* cause-and-effect reasoning;
* constraint identification;
* conflict analysis;
* assumption checking;
* refusal to hide behind "I know";
* search for options where more sides can win;
* feedback from reality.

## Core Choice Rules

### 1. Do Not Start With Options

Do not ask first:

```text
Which option do I like?
```

Ask:

```text
What goal are we trying to achieve?
What is blocking movement toward it?
```

### 2. Find The Constraint

Most choices are noisy because the real constraint is unclear.

If the constraint is wrong, the choice will optimize a non-bottleneck.

Current BrainCoach example:

```text
Constraint:
Payment Trigger Discovery

Non-constraints:
content volume
visual quality
automation depth
```

### 3. Treat Conflict As A Map Of Assumptions

Conflict should not be treated as proof that one side is bad.

Conflict usually means that two necessary needs appear incompatible because hidden assumptions are controlling the situation.

Agent question:

```text
Which assumption makes these two needs look incompatible?
```

### 4. Never Say "I Know" Too Early

"I know" can stop inquiry before reality has been checked.

Agents must mark:

* what is observed;
* what is inferred;
* what is assumed;
* what is still unknown;
* what would change the conclusion.

### 5. Prefer Win-Win Search Before Compromise

Compromise may be useful, but it can also preserve the wrong assumption.

Before compromise, agents must search for a better option:

```text
What change would allow both important needs to be satisfied?
```

### 6. Emotions Are Signals, Not Final Decisions

Emotion may reveal pressure, fear, resistance, urgency, or value.

Emotion should not be ignored.

Emotion should not be allowed to replace logic.

Agent question:

```text
What does this emotion signal about the system?
```

### 7. Choice Must Produce Feedback

A choice is incomplete until it defines how reality will answer.

Every choice must state:

* expected effect;
* observable signal;
* feedback window;
* correction rule.

## Agent Choice Protocol

When an agent recommends a choice, it must answer:

1. What is the goal?
2. What is the current reality?
3. What is the constraint?
4. What options are visible?
5. What conflict or contradiction is present?
6. What assumptions make the conflict exist?
7. Which assumption is most testable?
8. What option becomes possible if that assumption is false?
9. What is the expected effect?
10. What feedback from reality will confirm, weaken, or correct the choice?

## BrainCoach Application

### Interview To GPS

Bad choice frame:

```text
Should we push GPS harder?
```

Better choice frame:

```text
What prevents parents from seeing GPS as valuable?
```

Current interpretation:

Parents do not reject tracking in general.

They do not yet see how GPS helps them answer the payment-trigger question:

```text
Is my investment in the child becoming real progress?
```

Choice:

```text
Do not sell GPS as the offer.
Use GPS as the data mechanism behind a 14-day Trajectory Review.
```

Expected feedback:

* GPS CTA clicks;
* first tracker entries;
* Day 3 activity;
* Day 7 activity;
* parent replies describing clearer trajectory visibility.

### Content Production

Bad choice frame:

```text
Which content should we produce?
```

Better choice frame:

```text
Which market pain and payment trigger are we testing?
```

Choice rule:

Content is chosen by its ability to test reality, not by whether it is intellectually interesting.

## Relation To Anti-Noise Rule

Choice must not follow mass opinion automatically.

Mass opinion can reveal market language.

Mass opinion cannot replace:

* fact;
* закономерность;
* logic;
* measure;
* feedback from reality.

Agents must not choose the most common explanation if a fact, contradiction, or tested закономерность points elsewhere.

## Relation To TRIZ

Choice Practice identifies goal, constraint, conflict, and assumptions.

TRIZ Contradiction Practice helps generate a stronger option when visible choices produce a weak tradeoff.

If a choice contains:

```text
we want X
but X worsens Y
```

then the agent must use TRIZ before recommending compromise.
