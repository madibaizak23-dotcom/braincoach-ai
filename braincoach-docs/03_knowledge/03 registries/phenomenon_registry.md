# Purpose

Phenomenon Registry stores observed human phenomena so that BrainCoach accumulates research memory rather than isolated opinions.

The registry connects reality, observation, signals, hypotheses, experiments, outcomes, mechanisms, and models into a repeatable research process.

Its purpose is not content production.

Its purpose is understanding how humans learn, adapt, decide, perform, and change.

---

# Principles

* One phenomenon = one observable human pattern, anomaly, paradox, or recurring behavior.
* A phenomenon is recorded before explanation, not after.
* Every phenomenon must originate from one or more signals.
* Every phenomenon must generate a question.
* Every phenomenon must contain a hypothesis, validation method, and outcome.
* Registry stores both findings and the path that produced them.
* If a phenomenon does not improve understanding of people, its priority is low.
* If a phenomenon cannot be tested, it remains a hypothesis.
* A phenomenon can evolve across seasons.

# Fields

## PH-ID

Unique phenomenon identifier.

Example:

PH-001

---

## Title

Short phenomenon name.

---

## Short Description

One sentence describing what is observed.

---

## Source Signals

Signals that led to the discovery of the phenomenon.

Links to:

* Signal Registry
* Event Registry
* Observation Log

---

## Observation

Direct observation without premature interpretation.

What happened?

---

## Contradiction

Difference between expectation and reality.

Why is this surprising?

---

## Question

Research question generated by the phenomenon.

---

## Hypothesis

Testable explanation.

What may be causing the phenomenon?

---

## Experiment

Planned validation in reality.

How will we test it?

---

## Outcome

Observed result.

What happened after testing?

---

## Mechanism

Stable explanation supported by evidence.

How does the phenomenon work?

---

## Evidence Level

Current level of validation.

Allowed values:

* anecdotal
* observed
* repeated
* validated

---

## Confidence

Confidence in the mechanism.

Range:

0–100

---

## World

Context where the phenomenon appears.

Examples:

* learning
* school
* sport
* family
* work
* entrepreneurship
* technology
* relationships

---

## Cognitive Shift

The new understanding that emerges from the phenomenon.

One sentence only.

---

## Applications

Where the phenomenon should be used.

Examples:

* reels
* carousel
* stories
* thread
* article
* workshop
* consultation
* diagnostic

---

## Season

Research season linked to the phenomenon.

Example:

---

## Status

Allowed values:

* draft
* observed
* testing
* validated
* canonical
* revised
* rejected
* archived

---

## Next Experiment

Next validation step.

What still needs to be tested?

# Rules

* Do not confuse phenomenon with explanation.
* Do not delete phenomena; change status instead.
* Do not duplicate the same phenomenon under different names.
* Do not treat opinions as phenomena.
* Do not treat a single successful test as proof.
* Do not declare a mechanism validated without sufficient repetition.
* Every phenomenon must be linked to at least one signal.
* Every canonical phenomenon should eventually contribute to a model.

---

# Strategic Value

Phenomenon Registry becomes the most valuable layer of the BrainCoach Knowledge System.

It stores empirical memory.

Over time it enables:

* better research
* stronger diagnostics
* more accurate models
* season planning
* content generation from reality
* pattern recognition across domains

The goal is not to collect phenomena.

The goal is to accumulate understanding.
